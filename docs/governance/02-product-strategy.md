## Vision

Over five years, MyEventLane will become a trusted Australian home for people who bring communities together: a calm, capable path from an event idea to discovery, booking, event-day operation, follow-up and the next event.

Growth will deepen this purpose. It will not turn MyEventLane into a generic administration suite or a crowded global marketplace.

## Market

MyEventLane operates across event discovery, free registration, paid ticketing, organiser operations and event promotion. Its initial market is Australia, with particular relevance to local, grassroots, cultural, educational, charitable, independent and community-led events.

The market contains large discovery marketplaces, low-fee ticketing tools and polished invitation products. MyEventLane will compete through the coherence of the whole journey, the care given to free and paid events, trustworthy local operation and guidance that reduces organiser uncertainty.

## Target users

Primary users are:

- first-time and occasional organisers;
- volunteer and grassroots groups;
- independent creators, educators and community leaders;
- charities, associations and local organisations; and
- growing event businesses that need dependable tools without enterprise complexity.

Attendees are equally important participants in the journey. They need to find, understand, trust, book and attend events with minimal friction. Platform staff need safe, accountable tools to support both groups.

## Competitive positioning

MyEventLane is a community-first event platform for simple RSVPs, trusted local ticketing and guided organiser operations.

It is distinguished not by the largest feature list, but by making the next right step clear across the event lifecycle. It should be capable enough to manage money and live events, yet human enough to support a volunteer organising their first gathering.

## Product principles

Strategy must:

- begin with an organiser or attendee outcome;
- support confidence, community and momentum together;
- treat free RSVP and paid ticketing as complete journeys;
- connect creation, publishing, discovery, booking, attendance and follow-up;
- use progressive disclosure and plain language;
- protect trust, privacy, security, accessibility and payment integrity;
- work in real mobile and event-day conditions; and
- choose fewer coherent capabilities over disconnected breadth.

## Differentiators

1. **Guided event lifecycle** - one coherent workspace that changes with the event without forcing the organiser to relearn it.
2. **Equal product care** - free, paid, small and grassroots events receive dependable core experiences.
3. **Community-first discovery** - relevance, place and trust matter more than paid prominence alone.
4. **Transparent commerce** - prices, fees, payouts, refunds and status are explained before commitment.
5. **Operational confidence** - event-day tools remain clear under time pressure and interrupted attention.
6. **Responsible intelligence** - automation assists judgement without removing consent, authorship or accountability.

## Success measures

Success is assessed through a balanced set of evidence:

- first-time organisers reaching a valid draft and published event;
- completion and recovery rates across RSVP and paid booking;
- clear comprehension of event, price, status and next step;
- mobile completion and accessibility conformance;
- reliable payments, payouts, refunds, tickets and check-in;
- fewer support contacts caused by product confusion;
- repeat organising and useful post-event follow-up;
- participation by underserved communities;
- organiser and attendee trust;
- sustainable revenue, retention and cost to serve; and
- system reliability and incident recovery.

No single activity or revenue measure defines success.

## Strategic goals

### Years 1-2: Establish trust and coherence

- Make creation, publishing, discovery, booking and confirmation dependable.
- Establish Vendor Studio as the organiser's coherent event workspace.
- Bring free RSVP and paid ticketing to equal quality.
- Make mobile, accessibility, support and operational readiness measurable.
- Establish trusted analytics without unnecessary surveillance.

### Years 2-3: Strengthen organiser capability

- Connect promotion, audience communication, event-day operation and follow-up.
- Help organisers understand performance and improve future events.
- Support growing teams with clear permissions and shared responsibility.
- Develop sustainable paid value without withholding essential community capability.

### Years 3-5: Grow the community network

- Improve local and interest-based discovery while protecting fairness and safety.
- Build responsible community signals and repeat-attendance pathways.
- Extend mobile capability where it materially improves event-day work.
- Consider new regions or segments only when local trust, operations and compliance are mature.

## Deliberate exclusions

We will not pursue:

- growth based on hidden fees, forced urgency or attention extraction;
- feature parity for its own sake;
- enterprise complexity that compromises the primary community experience;
- advertising models that distort discovery or exploit personal data;
- social mechanics that turn community participation into a popularity game;
- opaque algorithmic ranking;
- automation that publishes, charges, refunds, messages or moderates without appropriate human control;
- native mobile applications merely for market appearance;
- uncontrolled international expansion;
- unrelated venue, workforce, CRM or accounting suites; or
- bespoke one-off workflows that fragment the product.

Opportunities outside these boundaries require a constitutional review, not an informal exception.
