## Foreword

MyEventLane began with a simple conviction: communities are built through events.

An event can be a gathering of ten people in a neighbourhood hall or thousands of people in a city venue. It can raise funds, share knowledge, celebrate identity, create belonging, launch an idea or simply give people a reason to be together. The size of the event does not determine its value. The people it brings together do.

Yet the people who make these moments possible are too often asked to work around systems that are complicated, expensive, impersonal or designed for organisations with more time, money and technical confidence than they have.

MyEventLane exists to change that.

This manifesto is the founding document of the MyEventLane product. It states why the product exists, whom it serves, what it believes and how it must behave. It is not a campaign, a slogan or a description of the current interface. It is a lasting standard against which the product must be judged.

Technology will change. Features will change. The needs of organisers and communities will continue to evolve. These commitments must remain.

## Why MyEventLane Exists

Events do more than fill calendars. They create the places where community becomes real.

They are where people meet, participate, learn, celebrate, organise, perform, support one another and feel that they belong. Behind every event is an organiser carrying responsibility: shaping an idea, making decisions, earning trust, welcoming people and keeping the event moving.

Too many event platforms make that work harder than it needs to be. They expose their own complexity. They overwhelm people with options before giving them direction. They treat organisers as account records, attendees as transactions and community events as inventory.

MyEventLane exists because organisers deserve better.

They deserve tools that respect their time, strengthen their confidence and help them move from an idea to a successful event without needing to understand the machinery underneath.

They deserve a platform that recognises that a free community gathering matters just as much to its people as a major paid event.

They deserve a product that helps them take the next right step.

## Purpose

MyEventLane exists to help people bring communities together through events.

Our purpose is to remove unnecessary friction from creating, publishing, promoting and running an event, while giving organisers the clarity and confidence to make good decisions.

We serve the organiser so the organiser can serve their community.

## Vision

Our vision is a world in which anyone with a worthwhile reason to bring people together can do so with confidence.

We want local organisers, grassroots groups, independent creators, community organisations and growing event businesses to have access to tools that feel capable, fair and genuinely made for them.

MyEventLane should become the trusted lane between an organiser's idea and a community's experience.

## Mission

Our mission is to provide a calm, welcoming and trustworthy event platform that guides organisers from first idea to successful event, and helps attendees find, trust, book and attend with ease.

We do this by:

- making event creation clear and achievable;
- supporting free RSVPs and paid ticketing without treating one as less important;
- helping organisers understand what needs attention and what to do next;
- making public event experiences easy to trust and act upon;
- supporting the full event journey, not merely the transaction; and
- building with communities rather than making assumptions about them.

## Three Product Pillars

### 1. Confidence

The organiser should always understand where they are, what is happening and what to do next.

Confidence comes from clear language, visible progress, dependable behaviour, honest feedback and one meaningful primary action at a time. It does not come from displaying every available feature.

When the product knows something important, it should say so. When the organiser must make a decision, the product should explain the consequence. When something has succeeded, the product should make the outcome unmistakable.

### 2. Community

Every product decision begins with the people an event brings together.

MyEventLane must work for small and emerging organisers as well as experienced ones. It must respect free, paid, social, cultural, educational, charitable, professional and grassroots events. It must make inclusion, accessibility, safety and belonging part of the product's foundations, not optional decoration.

Community is not a market segment. It is the reason the product exists.

### 3. Momentum

Every meaningful interaction should help the organiser move forward.

MyEventLane should reduce hesitation, reveal the next useful action and keep work connected across the event lifecycle. The product should remember progress, prevent avoidable repetition and turn completed work into a clear next step.

We do not measure momentum by how many screens a person visits. We measure it by whether they are closer to a successful event.

## Our Beliefs

### Communities deserve great tools

Community organisers should not receive a lesser product because their event is small, free, local or volunteer-led. Good design, dependable technology and respectful support should not be reserved for large organisations.

### Confidence is more valuable than features

A long feature list is not success. A person knowing what to do next is.

We would rather provide a smaller number of coherent, dependable capabilities than a crowded collection of tools that leave organisers uncertain.

### Technology should disappear

Organisers should not need to understand Drupal, Commerce, payment architecture, publishing states or internal product language to run an event.

The system may be sophisticated underneath. The experience should feel simple, natural and human.

### Every click should move the organiser forward

An action should create progress, reveal useful information or support a clear decision. Interactions that merely expose the structure of the system are not enough.

If a click does not help an organiser understand, decide, create, publish, promote, operate or learn, we must question why it exists.

### We design workflows, not pages

An organiser experiences one continuous journey, not a collection of routes, forms and modules.

Every surface must connect what came before with what happens next. A page is successful only when it contributes to the whole event journey.

## Product Philosophy

MyEventLane is an organiser operating system, not a collection of administration screens.

The product must translate complexity into guidance. It should lead people to the next step, ask the right question at the right time, present a clear solution and make the value of the next action easy to understand. Once an outcome is complete, it should invite the organiser back into the next useful stage of their event.

We favour:

- clarity over cleverness;
- confidence over feature density;
- guidance over instruction manuals;
- connected journeys over isolated pages;
- progressive disclosure over visible complexity;
- plain language over system language;
- prevention over recovery;
- trustworthy feedback over decorative celebration;
- established patterns over unnecessary variation; and
- real organiser needs over internal convenience.

The product should feel calm even when the work is complex. It should feel warm without becoming childish, professional without becoming corporate, and confident without becoming forceful.

## Workspace Philosophy

The MyEventLane Workspace is the organiser's place of truth for one event.

It is not a dashboard of everything the system knows. It is a working environment that helps the organiser understand the event's current state and act with confidence.

The Workspace follows four constitutional zones:

1. **Identity** - What event am I working on, and what is its current state?
2. **Guidance** - What matters now, and what should I do next?
3. **Work** - Where do I complete the task in front of me?
4. **Outcome** - What happened, what does it mean, and where can I go next?

The shell should remain familiar throughout the event lifecycle. The organiser should not have to relearn the product as an event moves from draft to live, from selling to doors open, or from finished to follow-up.

Mission Control provides guidance, not noise. The Launch Centre supports a deliberate publishing decision, not a technical state change. Success states do more than celebrate: they confirm the outcome and open the next useful path.

One event. One coherent workspace. One clear next step.

## Design Principles

### Soft, not weak

Use warmth, space, rounded forms and gentle surfaces, while keeping hierarchy, language, actions and boundaries strong.

### Trust before flair

Identity, price, payment, privacy, permissions, status and consequences must be clear before decorative expression is considered.

### One action per moment

Each state needs one obvious primary action. Secondary choices must remain available without competing for attention.

### Mobile first, always

Core journeys must work from the smallest supported screen. Desktop may enhance the experience; it must not define it.

### Community polished

The product should be friendly and human while remaining credible enough to hold personal information, manage money and support a live event.

### Accessible by default

Accessibility is a design constraint from the beginning. It includes perceivable content, understandable language, keyboard and assistive technology support, sufficient contrast, useful focus states, reduced-motion respect and touch targets that work in real conditions.

### Fewer, stronger patterns

We reuse proven components and interaction patterns. We do not create new variations to solve problems that an established pattern already solves.

### Meaning before decoration

Colour, illustration, animation and emphasis must communicate purpose. If a visual element does not improve understanding, orientation, trust or momentum, it should not compete with the work.

## Product Principles

1. **Start with the organiser's goal.** Define the human outcome before defining the feature.
2. **Make the next step visible.** No organiser should need to search the interface to understand what matters now.
3. **Ask only what is needed.** Request information at the point it becomes useful, and explain why it matters.
4. **Show consequences before commitment.** Publishing, charging, refunding, cancelling, deleting and messaging must never rely on surprise.
5. **Preserve progress.** Save work safely, make state visible and avoid asking people to repeat completed work.
6. **Use plain language.** Speak in the language of events and communities, never in the language of the underlying system.
7. **Keep one source of truth.** Do not create competing workflows, duplicate controls or contradictory states.
8. **Design the complete journey.** Creation, publishing, discovery, booking, confirmation, attendance, follow-up and learning must connect.
9. **Earn trust continuously.** Security, privacy, access, payment integrity and reliable feedback are product responsibilities.
10. **Support real conditions.** Design for mobile devices, interrupted attention, volunteer teams, changing event states and the pressure of event day.
11. **Measure outcomes, not activity.** More clicks, screens, components or features are not evidence of a better product.
12. **Invite the organiser forward.** Every completed outcome should make the next useful action clear without manufacturing urgency.

## What We Will Not Build

We will not build:

- a generic software-as-a-service administration tool;
- an Eventbrite clone;
- a product that feels like Drupal to the people using it;
- a feature wall that asks organisers to find their own path;
- competing dashboards or duplicate ways to perform the same core task;
- workflows organised around modules, entities or internal team structures;
- dark patterns, artificial urgency or manipulative selling;
- hidden fees, ambiguous payment states or unclear commercial consequences;
- complexity for the appearance of power;
- decoration that weakens clarity, accessibility or trust;
- engagement mechanics that treat community participation like a game;
- automation that removes necessary human judgement or consent;
- tools that privilege large paid events while neglecting free and grassroots gatherings;
- inaccessible experiences treated as acceptable temporary versions; or
- features without a clear relationship to an organiser or attendee outcome.

This does not mean MyEventLane will remain small. It means growth must deepen the product's purpose rather than dilute it.

## Success Measures

MyEventLane succeeds when people can bring communities together with greater confidence and less avoidable effort.

We will look for evidence that:

- a first-time organiser can understand how to begin;
- an organiser can create and publish an event without learning the underlying system;
- organisers know what requires attention and what can wait;
- free RSVP and paid ticket journeys both feel complete and respected;
- attendees can quickly understand what an event is, when and where it happens, what it costs and what to do next;
- people can trust booking, payment, confirmation, ticket and refund states;
- mobile users can complete every core journey;
- accessibility is sustained across design and implementation;
- organisers spend less time recovering from confusion or duplicated work;
- event-day tasks remain clear under pressure;
- completed events lead naturally to useful follow-up and future events;
- support needs reveal opportunities to improve the product rather than becoming accepted friction; and
- communities that have traditionally been underserved can use the platform safely and confidently.

Commercial health matters because MyEventLane must be sustainable. It is not, however, the sole definition of success. Revenue that depends on confusion, exclusion or unnecessary friction is not product success.

## Organiser Promise

To every organiser, MyEventLane promises:

- we will respect your time;
- we will explain what matters in plain language;
- we will not make you learn our technology to use your own event;
- we will show you where you are and help you understand what comes next;
- we will treat your free community gathering with the same product care as a paid event;
- we will protect the trust placed in us when you manage people, information and money;
- we will not hide important consequences behind vague language;
- we will design for the reality of organising, including uncertainty, interruption and event-day pressure; and
- we will keep listening as your needs and communities evolve.

## Governance

This manifesto governs product decisions across strategy, research, design, content, engineering, commerce, operations and support.

Every proposal must identify:

1. the organiser or attendee outcome it serves;
2. the manifesto principle or belief that supports it;
3. the next step it makes clearer;
4. the complexity, risk or duplication it introduces;
5. its effect on accessibility, trust, privacy, security and mobile use; and
6. how success will be observed or measured.

When choices conflict, the following order of authority applies:

1. The MyEventLane Organiser Manifesto
2. Product Constitution
3. Product Strategy and Product Requirements
4. Product Design System
5. Workspace Zones
6. Visual Language
7. Component Catalogue
8. Approved design decisions and assurance records
9. Implementation

Lower-level documents may interpret this manifesto. They may not contradict it.

Existing behaviour is not automatically correct because it already exists. A technically convenient solution is not automatically acceptable because it is easier to implement. A competitor pattern is not automatically appropriate because it is familiar.

Decisions with a material effect on the organiser journey must record their manifesto alignment. If a proposal cannot show that alignment, it must be revised, deferred or rejected.

## Constitutional Status

This manifesto is the highest product authority for MyEventLane.

It applies to current and future products, regardless of platform, technology, architecture or commercial model. It sits above the Product Design System, Workspace Zones, Visual Language, Component Catalogue and implementation.

Changes to this document must be deliberate, rare and transparent. They require Product Owner approval, a written reason, an assessment of the effect on existing product commitments and a recorded version change.

No feature, deadline, technical limitation, commercial opportunity or individual preference may quietly override this manifesto.

Where another MyEventLane document conflicts with this one, this manifesto prevails.

## Our Promise

We will build MyEventLane around the courage it takes to bring people together.

We will make complex work feel understandable. We will guide without controlling, support without overwhelming and help organisers move forward without losing sight of the people their events are for.

We will treat trust as something earned in every interaction.

## Our Commitment

We commit to building a product that helps organisers create belonging.

We will listen carefully, decide honestly and design with purpose. We will measure our work by the confidence it gives people, the barriers it removes and the communities it helps bring together.

As MyEventLane grows, this commitment will remain its centre:

**Every organiser deserves a clear path. Every community deserves the chance to gather.**
