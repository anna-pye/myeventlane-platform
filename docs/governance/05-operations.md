# MyEventLane Operations

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Status | Permanent operational governance |
| Highest authority | [Organiser Manifesto](./00-organiser-manifesto.md) |

## Operating standard

Operations exists to keep MyEventLane safe, dependable and human in real conditions. Procedures must respect people's time, explain consequences, preserve evidence and lead to a clear next step.

## Support

Support is accessible, respectful and accountable. Requests are prioritised by harm, time sensitivity and event impact rather than customer value alone. Ownership, expected next update and resolution are visible. Repeated support needs are treated as product evidence.

Staff access only the information needed to resolve the request. Actions affecting identity, orders, tickets, money or messages require an auditable reason.

## Moderation

Moderation protects participation, safety and lawful use while preserving procedural fairness. Reports receive acknowledgement, risk triage, proportionate action and a record of evidence. People are told what decision was made and what review path exists, except where disclosure would create harm or breach legal duties.

Automation may prioritise review; accountable human judgement remains necessary for material decisions.

## Refunds

Refund terms and decision authority are shown before purchase and available afterwards. Requests have a clear status, owner and expected next step. Refund actions must preserve order and payment evidence, prevent duplicate payment movement and communicate the outcome to affected parties.

Disputes between organiser policy, platform obligations and consumer rights are escalated rather than hidden behind system limitations.

## Payments

Payments, fees, payouts, holds and failures are reconciled against authoritative records. Access follows least privilege and separation of duties. Manual financial intervention requires documented authority, reason and verification. Ambiguous states are treated as incidents until resolved.

## Incident response

Incidents are classified by harm to people, money, privacy, security, access and event continuity. Response follows:

1. acknowledge and establish ownership;
2. contain harm without destroying evidence;
3. maintain an incident record and decision log;
4. communicate honestly at an appropriate cadence;
5. recover and verify service;
6. support affected people; and
7. conduct a blameless review with owned follow-up.

Event-day incidents receive heightened urgency. Speed does not permit silent or unsafe state changes.

## Security

Security is a continuing product and operational responsibility. MyEventLane applies least privilege, defence in depth, secure defaults, dependency and vulnerability management, protected secrets, logging, recovery and proportionate review.

Suspected compromise is escalated promptly. Evidence is preserved and access is limited to responders. Security work must not expose sensitive details unnecessarily.

## Privacy

MyEventLane collects and retains only information needed for a defined purpose. People receive understandable notice and meaningful choices. Access, use, sharing, export, correction and deletion follow law, stated policy and operational controls.

Analytics and automation are subject to the same privacy standard as visible features. Production personal information is never used casually for testing.

## Community standards

Standards protect dignity, safety, inclusion, lawful participation and the ability of communities to gather. They apply consistently to organisers, attendees, staff and partners. Enforcement is proportionate, documented and reviewable.

Community standards do not become a substitute for listening to affected communities or responding to local context.

## Accessibility

Accessibility is part of normal operations, not a later compliance exercise. Support channels, incident communication, policies and operational tools must be accessible. Reported barriers are triaged as product defects with severity based on blocked outcomes and available alternatives.

Release processes may not accept an inaccessible core journey as an ordinary temporary state.

## Documentation

Operational documentation has an owner, audience, status, last review date and next review trigger. Procedures are task-based, plain-language and tested in representative conditions. Sensitive runbooks have controlled access.

Documentation records authoritative behaviour; it does not quietly create policy. When product behaviour and documentation differ, the risk is recorded and resolved.

## Release management

Every release has:

- defined scope and accountable owner;
- reviewed product and Manifesto alignment;
- proportionate testing and accessibility evidence;
- security, privacy, data and payment impact assessment;
- deployment, verification and recovery instructions;
- operational and support readiness; and
- release notes for affected audiences.

High-risk changes are staged and observed. A release is complete only when production behaviour is verified and operational ownership is active.

## Operational learning

Support patterns, incidents, moderation appeals, refund disputes, accessibility reports and failed releases feed product discovery. Learning is recorded without blaming individuals or normalising avoidable friction.
